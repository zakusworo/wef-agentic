# Fit assessment

**Recommendation: submit an EOI.** The project directly matches Part V's topic
“AI for Sustainable Agriculture, Water, and Energy Systems.” Secondary connections
are governance of AI for climate action and conceptual links between climate literacy
and sustainability. This is a scope assessment, not an estimate of acceptance odds.

The [September official call](https://istic-unesco.org/v3/2026/09/08/call-for-book-chapters-artificial-intelligence-for-climate-learning-and-sustainable-development/)
requests a title, author details and a 150–300-word abstract by 21 September 2026.
The supplied [brochure](../source/Call-for-contribution_08092026.PDF) accepts practice-based
and policy-oriented chapters, lists peer review, no publication fee, and expected
publication in 2027. Follow the September call rather than the earlier April announcement.

## Positioning

Present a reproducible learning workflow in which readers inspect how climate,
irrigation, land use and energy assumptions change connected outcomes. Explain the
role of each agent and the boundary between computed quantities and generated
interpretation. Use Sleman as a contextual demonstration, with honest input limitations.

The educational contribution needs explicit learning activities: identifying source
quality, tracing a cross-sector consequence, comparing scenarios, and challenging
an AI-generated recommendation. A software screenshot alone does not establish
climate literacy gains. No learner or stakeholder evaluation has been conducted.

## Evidence available and missing

| Claim | Current support | Permissible wording |
|---|---|---|
| Runnable demonstrator | Streamlit startup and browser checks; recorded full Claude run | Implemented demonstration |
| Working numerical/agent pipeline | Offline regression suite and saved live run | Software verification, not local calibration |
| Five-scenario comparison | Saved deterministic synthetic sweep | Illustrative comparison under stated assumptions |
| Learning effectiveness | No participant study | Proposed evaluation / intended learning use |
| Accurate local prediction | Pump/irrigation assumptions remain uncalibrated | Screening model with limitations |
| Community or policy impact | No workshop or adoption evidence | Potential application, not demonstrated impact |

Before a full chapter, complete real-climate experiments, audit source quantities,
check sensitivity convergence, and manually assess narrative fidelity against the
saved numbers. If feasible, evaluate the learning activity with participants under
appropriate consent. Otherwise frame the chapter as a practice-based design and
demonstration, explicitly reserving learning-outcome claims for future work.

EOI preparation does not require completion of SWAT+, OSeMOSYS, AquaCrop, CMIP6 or
the entire Phase 2/3 roadmap. A focused, completed contribution is preferable to
promising every planned integration.
