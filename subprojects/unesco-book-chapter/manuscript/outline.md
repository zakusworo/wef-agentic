# Proposed chapter structure

Length and formatting will follow the editors' guidelines if the EOI is selected.

1. **Learning problem and Sleman context.** Explain why cross-sector reasoning and
   source awareness matter; cite primary local evidence and avoid generalizing from
   one district to the whole Global South.
2. **Design and accountability.** Define the five-agent workflow, deterministic
   calculations, provenance, critic role and limits of the term “agentic.”
3. **Models, data and assumptions.** State equations, periods, spatial scale,
   parameter sources, synthetic/reanalysis distinction and unresolved calibration.
4. **Learning activity.** Learners predict a consequence, compare scenarios, inspect
   assumptions, challenge a generated claim, and explain a trade-off in their own words.
5. **Demonstration and experiments.** Include architecture, seasonal water balance,
   scenario comparisons, source-quality checks, narrative fidelity and sensitivity.
6. **Evaluation.** Separate software tests, model validation and learning evaluation.
   Report only completed work as results.
7. **Transfer and limitations.** Data scarcity, language/access needs, operating costs,
   AI footprint uncertainty, local validation and human review.

## Evaluation protocol to develop

- Define learners (e.g. students or local planning staff) and prerequisites before recruitment.
- Use tasks assessing causal explanation, uncertainty recognition and source checking.
- Retain a baseline activity without AI interpretation for a meaningful comparison.
- Define scoring and assessment procedure before collecting responses.
- Obtain consent and any required institutional review; avoid collecting unnecessary identifiers.
- Report sample, attrition, limitations and disagreement. Do not fabricate participant responses.

Until participants are studied, describe these steps as a proposed protocol and make
no claim that the tool improves literacy or decisions.
